MULE ACCOUNT TRACER

Follow the scam money.
Freeze it before it's cashed out.

A Java engine that takes a fraud complaint, traces the victim's money hop by hop through layered mule accounts in time order, and tells banks exactly which accounts to freeze first.

Scenario · "Digital arrest"

A retired professor in Chennai gets a video call from a fake "CBI officer". He's told his Aadhaar is linked to money laundering and he must move his savings to an "RBI verification account". Over 52 minutes he makes 4 transfers totalling ₹18.5 lakh. The gang splits the money across 21 mule accounts and cashes it out through ATMs in Jamtara, Nuh and Bharatpur and USDT P2P desks.
Synthetic data generated by the Java simulator (seed 1930). Real banking data is never used.

2 · Every minute counts

Recoverable share vs. complaint delay

Accuracy against ground truth

The simulator knows which accounts really are mules, so we can grade the engine's decisions.

Hard actions = FREEZE + BLOCK. Accounts that are likely innocent only get a LIEN on the exact tainted amount, so a shopkeeper paid ₹1,200 by a mule can keep using the rest of his account.

3 · Prioritised freeze list sent to banks

#AccountBank · CityHopHeld nowScoreActionEvidence

Ordered by a max-heap on held × risk, so the fraud desk acts first where a freeze recovers the most money with the least chance of hitting an innocent account.

4 · Mule rings & pre-emptive watchlist

Rings found via shared devices / networks (union-find)

Chokepoints (minimum vertex cut, Dinic max-flow)

The fewest accounts that every observed route from the victim to a cash-out point passed through. Blocking them shuts this gang's pipeline, so the next victim's money has nowhere to go.

Watchlist: dormant ring accounts

These accounts haven't touched this victim's money, but they log in from the same phones as the mules. Flag them before the next victim pays them.

Temporal graph

Each account's debits stay sorted by time. Money that arrived at 10:04 can only leave on a debit after 10:04, found by binary search.

insert O(1) amortised · lookup O(log d)

Taint propagation

An event-driven k-way merge: a min-heap holds one cursor per tainted account. A debit carries min(amount, tainted balance) onward. Money is conserved exactly.

O((S + E′) log K)

Mule rings

Incremental union-find over device fingerprints and IP hashes. Member lists merge on union, so a query touches only the rings that were hit.

O(α(n)) per login event

Risk score

7 explainable signals: concentration, pass-through, speed, ring, account age, PageRank hub, fan-out. Behaviour that hasn't been observed yet is excluded, not counted as innocent.

PageRank O(iter·(V+E))

Chokepoints

Node-split flow network (capacity 1 per mule) solved with Dinic. By Menger's theorem, the min cut equals the number of vertex-disjoint cash-out routes.

O(E·√V) on unit capacities

REST API

JDK HttpServer, no frameworks: /api/investigate?utr=…&reportTime=… returns the trail, freeze list, rings and cut as JSON.
